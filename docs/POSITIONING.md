## Brand Script (StoryBrand)

Live homepage score: **0/10**. The page introduces Mathias and lists React, mobile, and the web. The visitor is not the hero, no problem is named, and nothing asks them to write.

This script scores **7/10**. It has one desire, the internal problem, one direct call to action, and a repeatable one-liner. It does not yet have a proof point, a plan on the page, or a failure line on the page. Those three gaps are what a 10 would add. No client, number, or testimonial was invented.

- Character: a person who wants to work together.
- Desire: know whether Mathias is the right engineer, without a long courtship.
- External problem: the page does not show what kind of work the conversation is for.
- Internal problem: writing feels like a guess. Confirmed as the feeling the copy must name. Still not a visitor quote.
- Philosophical problem: you should be able to find out if it's a fit without guessing.
- Villain: a vague introduction.
- Guide, empathy: "If you can't tell whether I'm the right engineer..."
- Guide, authority: software engineer in Vienna. The work is React, mobile, and the web. The three projects are the only proof. A stronger proof line is still blocked on a real fact.
- Plan: 1. See the work. 2. Send one message. 3. Find out if it's a fit. This stays in the script. It is not a new homepage section.
- Agreement: one message is the whole commitment. No reply-time promise.
- Direct call to action: Contact.
- Transitional call to action: the project grid. No second hero button.
- Failure, not on the page: if you don't write, you're still guessing.
- Success: one message, and you know if you should work together.
